Where the hours go in a trade business

Trade businesses run on the same handful of systems: a job management platform like SimPRO or ServiceM8, Xero for the accounts, an inbox, and a phone. Each one works. The cost is in the gaps between them, where a person re-types the same job details three times and nobody counts the minutes.

That work is invisible on a P&L. It shows up as a bookkeeper's hours, as jobs that got missed, as invoices that went out late, and as an owner doing paperwork at 8pm.

Why trades are behind, and why that's an opportunity

Australia's National AI Centre put SME adoption at 43% across December 2025 to February 2026, with fewer than 30% of construction businesses using AI against more than half in the services sectors. The reason isn't capability: 54% of non-adopting businesses said AI simply wasn't relevant to them, which the Centre attributes to an absence of visible, relatable examples.

In other words, most of this admin is still being done by hand in trades, not because it can't be automated, but because nobody has shown what it looks like for a business like theirs.

What gets automated first

  • Job intake: inbound emails, forms and calls become jobs in your system, with the site address, fault and urgency already filled in.
  • Invoicing: invoices raised from completed job data instead of assembled by hand.
  • Quote follow-ups: the quote sent a fortnight ago that nobody chased.
  • Compliance reporting: structured documents generated the moment a job closes, in the right format every time.
  • Cross-system data entry: the same details typed into a second and third system.

What's the first thing a trade business should automate?

Usually inbound job intake: turning emails, forms and calls into jobs in your system. It is high volume, it happens before anything else can go wrong, and it is the step most often still done by hand.

Start where the volume is highest and the process is clearest, not where the technology is most interesting. Intake qualifies on both counts: the same few fields get typed over and over, and a missed request costs a job. Invoicing is usually second, being equally repetitive and tied directly to cash. What to leave alone early on is anything involving pricing judgment or a difficult customer conversation. If you don't know where your hours go, describe a normal week using the question form or by email, and Chris will reply with what looks worth automating first.

Will this work if I'm on the tools all day and not tech-savvy?

Yes. A working automation should be close to invisible. If you notice it at all, it's because a job appeared in your system without anyone typing it in, or a question arrived asking you to check one thing.

The build happens inside the systems you already use, so nothing new lands on your phone. Where a decision is genuinely needed, it arrives as an ordinary message with the original request attached, not as a dashboard you have to log into. The one thing that does need your time is the mapping conversation at the start, because the process lives in your head and has to be described before it can be built. That is a single conversation, not a training course.

What if my software doesn't have an API?

There is almost always a way in: a scheduled export, an emailed report, a database connection, or a screen-level tool as a last resort. The real question is whether the workaround is stable enough to depend on.

Modern cloud tools like SimPRO, ServiceM8, Xero and Microsoft 365 all have APIs, so this comes up mainly with older or industry-specific systems. Where none exists, the usual approach is a scheduled file export that the workflow picks up: reliable, but not instant. Screen-driven automation is possible and occasionally the right call, but it is brittle and worth avoiding unless the alternative is re-keying forever. Either way it gets tested against real data before anyone depends on it.
