ServiceM8 and Xero: what syncs automatically

Customers and items sync both ways, approved invoices are raised in Xero, and payments flow in both directions — a payment recorded in Xero reaches ServiceM8 within about half an hour. Tax rates and income account codes import from Xero, and in Australia staff shift times can go across as employee timesheets.

// written by Chris Olanda, founder of Kontrol AI · last reviewed August 2026

What syncs

  • Customers and items, both directions — a change in one system updates the other.
  • Invoices — approving an invoice in ServiceM8 raises it in Xero, ready to reconcile.
  • Payments, both directions. Payments taken against an invoice before approval go across when the invoice is exported; payments taken after approval go immediately; payments recorded in Xero come back to ServiceM8 within roughly 30 minutes.
  • Tax rates and income account codes, imported from Xero automatically.
  • Staff shift times as employee timesheets — Australia only.

For most small field-service operations that covers the accounting side properly, without custom work. If your complaint is that invoices take too long to get into Xero, the add-on already solves it.

Where the hours actually are

The common experience is that the systems are connected and the admin load hasn't moved. That is because the integration begins at the invoice, and the work sits before it.

  • An email or a missed call that should become a job, typed in by hand.
  • A quote sent a fortnight ago that nobody followed up.
  • Compliance paperwork assembled after a job closes.
  • The same job details re-entered into a third system that isn't ServiceM8 or Xero.

None of those are accounting problems, so no accounting integration will fix them. They are the steps that a workflow connecting your inbox, ServiceM8 and Xero can take off a person entirely.

What that looks like in practice

An inbound email arrives. A workflow reads it, works out it is a job request, pulls the site address, the fault and the urgency out of the text, and creates the job in ServiceM8 with the right client attached. Anything ambiguous goes to a person with the original email attached rather than being guessed at.

Kontrol AI's live client pipelines process jobs this way every day across job intake and invoicing, with current job counts, success rate and hours removed published on the home page and refreshed daily.

Common questions

// straight answers

Can ServiceM8 be connected to Xero automatically?

Yes. Customers and items sync both ways, approved invoices are raised in Xero, and payments flow in both directions — a payment recorded in Xero syncs back to ServiceM8 within about half an hour.

The ServiceM8 add-on also imports tax rates and income account codes from Xero, and in Australia it can send staff shift times across as employee timesheets. For most small trade operations that covers the accounting side without custom work. What it does not cover is everything before the invoice: the inbound email that should have become a job, the quote nobody followed up, the compliance paperwork attached to a finished job. That is usually where the hours actually go.

What doesn't the ServiceM8 Xero add-on cover?

Everything before the invoice. The add-on handles accounting sync well, but the inbound email that should have become a job, the quote nobody followed up, and the compliance paperwork attached to a finished job are all still manual.

That matters because those steps are usually where the hours actually go. Invoicing is already fast once a job is approved in ServiceM8 — the slow parts are reading a customer's email and creating the job, chasing a quote that went quiet, and assembling paperwork after the work is done. Those sit outside the accounting integration entirely, which is why businesses often report that their systems are connected and their admin load hasn't moved.

ServiceM8 or SimPRO — which suits my business?

Broadly, ServiceM8 suits smaller field-service operators who want jobs, quotes and invoices handled simply, while SimPRO suits larger contractors with project work, inventory, multi-stage jobs and more complex reporting.

The honest answer is that the right platform depends on how complicated your jobs are, not how big your business is. A five-person outfit doing complex project work may outgrow a simpler tool quickly; a twenty-person outfit doing straightforward service calls may never need more. Both connect to Xero, and both leave the same gap before the invoice. Switching platforms is a months-long project, so it is rarely the right first move — automating around what you already run usually is.
