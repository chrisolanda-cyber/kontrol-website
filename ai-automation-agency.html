What does an AI automation agency actually do?

Strip the language back and the job is specific: find the processes in a business where a person is re-typing information between systems, and replace that copying with a workflow that runs on its own — reliably, with a person still in charge of the judgment calls.

In practice that looks like an email that becomes a job in SimPRO without anyone keying it in, an invoicing run that assembles itself for approval instead of being built by hand, or a live dashboard that replaces the Friday spreadsheet ritual. The automation sits between the systems you already run; replacing your software is rarely part of the answer.

How is Kontrol AI different from a typical agency?

Three ways, and each one is checkable.

  • You deal with the builder. The practice is led by its founder, and the person in the review call is the person who designs, builds and documents the work. No account layer, no handoffs between a salesperson, a strategist and a delivery team who have never seen your process.
  • You own the result. The workflows are built on n8n — a standard, widely used automation platform — documented at handover, and able to be picked up by another developer at any time. Building inside a closed platform the client cannot access or export is a well-documented industry pattern; it does not happen here.
  • Accuracy is measured, not asserted. Kontrol AI's live client pipelines have recorded a 99.6% success rate across 1,002 automation runs and 728 jobs as at 3 August 2026, with the remainder surfaced as exceptions for a person to handle rather than hidden. Every run is logged, so any output can be traced back to the input that produced it.

How to compare automation agencies

Whoever you end up hiring, four questions separate the businesses that will still be running their automation in two years from the ones that will not:

  • Who owns the automation when the engagement ends? If the answer involves their platform and their login, keep looking.
  • What happens when a run fails? The right answer involves an alert to a person with the original input attached — silent failure is the real risk with automation, not failure itself.
  • What happens to the input that does not fit the pattern? A well-built workflow routes the ambiguous case to a human rather than guessing.
  • Can they show a measured success rate on live client work? Not a demo — logged runs against real data.

Those are the questions this practice is built to answer well, and you are welcome to put every one of them to us in the review.
